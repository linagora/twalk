# The search archive is the only long-term holder of third parties' words, and it never leaves the owner's session

The owner's personal archive — their mail, and later their messaging — is indexed **in full text** by the collector, in Tantivy, under the state directory it already writes its cursors and its grant into; the index is **encrypted at rest**, opened only with a key (`COLLECTOR_INDEX_KEY_FILE`) that lives as a **file on the host** and never in a variable, and it is **off by default**: no key, no index, nothing written, and the read answers `503 index_not_configured`. The index is reachable by **no persona** — it is not on the bus, it is not in a persona's environment, and the collector's internal `/search` route requires the service token no persona holds — and the Companion Gateway relays the owner's own read to the owner's own session (`GET /api/search`, behind the device-token guard) without ever parsing a hit or opening a body: it hands over **bounded snippets**, never a whole message. **It acts on nothing.** Searching is not deciding ([ADR 0042](0042-mail-triage-is-the-owners-rules-and-not-the-agents-judgement.md)): the index moves no mail, publishes no event, files nothing and deletes nothing. A correspondent the owner **revoked** has their hits **withdrawn** from the results, and the withdrawal is **counted** rather than hidden ([ADR 0012](0012-revoked-consent-reduces-publication.md)), because a silence the owner did not ask for is the one failure this product has shipped repeatedly without seeing it.

Rationale: [ADR 0028](0028-a-contacts-words-live-apart-from-the-event-that-identifies-them.md) states the rule this archive sits against — *the bus is the only place in the deployment where the plaintext of other people's messages exists at rest*, and it holds it seven days. An index of twenty-five years of mail is **the longest holder of third parties' words in the whole of Twalk**, the exact inverse of that rule, and it is a consequence of the archive existing rather than a defect in it: the archive is the owner's, it is about their own correspondence, and what they want from it is to find a message from three years ago without opening each client in turn. An archive nobody can search is not a safer archive, it is the same words on the same disk with less use. So the decision is not to shrink the archive but to **name what it is and guard it**: it exists only when the owner asks for it, it is ciphertext on the platter, its key is not the deployment's to keep, and no agent outside the owner's session can read it. This is the shape [ADR 0028](0028-a-contacts-words-live-apart-from-the-event-that-identifies-them.md) already gave the bus — words held apart, with a decision behind the holding — applied to the one surface that holds them longest, and it is why the key is a **file**: the lesson of [#239](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/239) is that a secret on a command line or in a committed `.env` is a secret published, and at-rest encryption material is exactly such a secret.

The limits are stated rather than papered over. **Twalk names the requirement and cannot enforce it**: it writes the index under `COLLECTOR_STATE_DIR` and refuses to open one without a key, and it is the deployment — the operator, through a `gocryptfs` mount or an encrypted filesystem — that makes the bytes unreadable, which the runbook in `deploy/README.md` says in as many words. **Encryption at rest protects a stolen disk, a snapshot, a backup; it does not protect a running process.** While the collector runs, the decrypted index is mounted and whoever can read that mount or the container's memory reads the owner's mail in plain text — an attacker already inside the deployment, or root on the host. That is not a gap this decision can close and it is not one it pretends to: it is the honest bound of "encrypted", and it is why the archive is also held to the session rule below, so that the *reachable* surface is no wider than the *stored* one. **A capability that holds years of other people's words does not switch itself on by omission**, so its absence is never a startup refusal either: no key means the index is off and the mail still flows, because turning an optional capability into a failure of the mail would be the wrong direction of danger twice over. And the archive is **not a second bus**: the backfill that fills it from before the deployment ran writes nothing on it ([ADR 0037](0037-the-bus-keeps-ninety-days-and-two-gigabytes-and-no-more.md)), so an index asked to reach back twenty-five years cannot overflow the stream that holds ninety days.

The costs are accepted. **One process holds the plaintext of the archive while it runs**, and that process is the collector, which already reads the owner's mailbox as the owner — so the archive widens what a compromise of that one component would yield, from the messages in flight to the whole history, and the at-rest encryption is what keeps that history from travelling with a copied disk. **The key is an operator secret** the deployment must keep and must not commit, one more file beside the OIDC client's secret, whose loss makes the archive unreadable and whose leak makes the encryption decorative. **The screened surface names correspondents** — a search result shows who wrote — which is the first place in the product a contact's name appears ([ADR 0012](0012-revoked-consent-reduces-publication.md) keeps it off the approval screen), and it is legitimate here for a reason worth writing down: the owner is searching **their own** messages in **their own** session, and showing them who wrote is the function of the screen rather than a leak; the *text* stays bounded to a snippet, never the body. None of this lets an agent see the archive ([ADR 0032](0032-twalk-governs-what-an-agent-outside-it-may-see-and-do.md)): the persona's environment is a closed allowlist and the service token is not in it, so the search is **a capability of the owner in their session**, not a tool of any agent — and if the owner should one day want an agent to search the archive, that is a **fourth governed read** with its own argument, its own journal and its own ADR, at exactly the cost [ADR 0039](0039-the-drafting-agent-is-graded-by-what-it-can-reach.md) sets, and never a widening of this one.
