# An OIDC grant is the user's identity, and a JMAP submission is their device

An approved reply to a mail leaves **from the owner's own mailbox**, submitted over JMAP with the access token the collector's OIDC grant renews — the token that already *is* the owner at the mail service — and with the owner's own JMAP identity as the sender ([#278](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/278)). No second credential is provisioned for the reply: the grant the operator gave once at the terminal ([#274](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/274)) is what reads the INBOX and what writes to Sent, because at the mail service those are one account and one right.

Rationale: this is [ADR 0025](0025-twalk-acts-as-the-user-through-a-device-of-their-account.md)'s decision, met on a different network. There, a mautrix bridge relays to its network only what the *logged-in user's own account* sends, so Twalk acts as the user through a device of their Matrix account — a second credential, provisioned by an operator route, because the Sensor's own account could observe but not speak for the user. Here the collector never had an account of its own: the grant is the user's from the first request, obtained by the user signing in as themselves, and the mail service knows no distinction between "the owner reading" and "the owner sending" beyond the scopes the grant carries. So the counterpart of the device is the **submission**: `EmailSubmission/set` from the owner's identity, which the service records as the owner sending — in Sent, in the Message-ID it mints, in the `From` every recipient sees. The reply is the owner's mail in every way a recipient or an auditor can check, which is what [ADR 0019](0019-a-persona-discloses-itself-to-the-contact.md)'s disclosure in the body is for: the mail is the owner's, and says a persona drafted it.

What the reply is, exactly, is decided here too, since a mail has choices a room does not. It goes to the **sender alone**: reply-all is a decision the owner did not take when they approved a suggestion produced for one person's message, and a persona's words reaching a list the owner did not review is the failure this project guards against everywhere else. It is **threaded**: `In-Reply-To` names the mail answered, `References` continues the mail's own, `Re:` is added once — so the recipient's client shows it where the conversation is. It is **plain text**, the approved body and nothing else: the contract's `final.format` is carried and `text/plain` is what a mail client renders identically everywhere; HTML is a later decision. It leaves a **copy in Sent**, because the owner's own mail client is where the owner expects to find what left under their name — a reply the owner cannot see in their own Sent folder is a reply they cannot account for.

Two things follow that the Sensor's shape already had. The **retry policy is the Sensor's** ([ADR 0022](0022-the-gateway-is-the-single-writer-of-approvals.md) made the Gateway the single writer of approvals and the outbound half a consumer of them): a submission the service refuses is dead-lettered with the reason, one the service does not answer is retried with a growing delay and then dead-lettered, and every sent reply is reported on the `.posted` subject with `reach=contact` — a mail from the owner's mailbox reaches the recipient by construction, which is the whole point, and a report that exists only on failure would make success a silence ([#216](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/216)). And **one subject, two senders**: the Sensor and the collector consume `persona.reply.approved.v1` on their own durable consumers and each acknowledges the other's targets untouched — a room is the Sensor's, a mail connection is the collector holding it — so no approval is dead-lettered for being the other's, and a deployment with two collectors (two accounts, two grants) has two consumers that leave each other alone.

The cost is the one [ADR 0025](0025-twalk-acts-as-the-user-through-a-device-of-their-account.md) named and the security model's residual risk 18 carries: the credential that reads the mailbox is the credential that sends from it, and there is no narrower grant to hold for the send. A leaked grant sends as the owner until the owner revokes it at the SSO. What bounds it is what bounds every send in this project — an approval needs a live, consented suggestion within the Gateway's window, so the grant cannot be made to write to an arbitrary address by a stranger who holds only the grant — and what makes it visible is the copy in Sent.
