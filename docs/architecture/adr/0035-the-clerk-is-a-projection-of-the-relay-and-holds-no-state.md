# The clerk is a projection of the relay and holds no state

The clerk — the Twalk component that writes onto the owner's Buzz relay what the bus says ([#265](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/265)) — **keeps no state of its own**: no database, no file, no map from a suggestion's id to the post it became. What it has already written it finds by **reading its own posts** and the **reference line** each one carries — `twalk:suggestion:<id> expires <when>`, the last line of the post, visible to the owner and not hidden in a tag — so that "has this suggestion been posted?" is a query of the relay for the clerk's own events in that channel with that line, and never a lookup in something the clerk remembers. A **redelivery and a restart are the same read**: the bus redelivering a trigger the clerk already handled, and the clerk starting fresh with a durable consumer pointed at history it has already consumed, are answered by the same query with the same result, one post, so neither can produce a second. The sweep that deletes an expired suggestion's post ([#219](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/219)) is the same shape from the other side: it reads the channel, reads each post's reference line, which names when its suggestion expires, and deletes what has passed — it consults no list of posts it made and asks the bus nothing. The relay is therefore the clerk's memory, and this is the shape the pending-contact projection ([#54](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/54)) and the portal register ([#105](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/105), ADR 0024) already have, taken one step further: those hold a narrow view and this holds none.

Rationale: the alternative was weighed and is the obvious one — a SQLite of suggestion id → post id beside the clerk, the way the Companion Gateway keeps its consent journal and its approval records — and it was rejected because of what it would make the relay. With a store, the relay becomes a **copy** of something Twalk holds, and the moment a post is deleted from Buzz Desktop by the owner, or lost by a relay rebuilt from an older backup, or never written because the relay refused it, the store and the relay disagree and nothing says which is true; the clerk would then have to reconcile, and reconciliation is what a projection exists to not need. ADR 0032 draws the line the other way: Buzz is a **surface** and never an authority, and a surface that Twalk keeps a shadow of is halfway to being one. There is also the plainer argument that this project has already paid for: the Gateway's suggestion listing ([#97](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/97)) is a projection of the bus and not a second store, on the ground that "a replay and the list cannot disagree with nobody able to say which is true" — and the clerk stands between two truths, the bus and the relay, so a third would be one more than the two it already has to keep consistent. What makes it possible without a store is that the relay is queryable by author and the reference line is a stable key: the suggestion's id is deterministic (it is also its `Nats-Msg-Id`), so the same suggestion names the same line from every delivery, and the clerk signs with a **key of its own** (a third identity beside the owner's and Hermes's, [#240](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/240)), so "my own posts" is a filter on one pubkey and no post of anybody else's can be mistaken for the clerk's memory. And a component with nothing to back up, nothing to migrate and nothing to reconcile is the one this deployment can afford to add: what the clerk brings to a host is one Nostr key, a row in a credential table, and no database in anybody's backup plan.

The costs are accepted rather than argued away. **One query per suggestion and one per sweep**, every time, against a relay whose rate limit is 300 requests a minute per key: a burst of suggestions on a busy day and a sweep every `CLERK_SWEEP_SECONDS` both draw on that budget, and a deployment that hits it sees posts delayed by the clerk's backoff rather than lost — bounded, counted (`twalk_clerk_relay_failures_total`), and the reason the sweep interval is configurable rather than fixed at a minute. **A reference line the owner sees**, on every post in `approbations`: a line of machinery in a channel meant for a human decision, which the ticket chose over a hidden tag deliberately — what the clerk reads to find its way, the owner reads too, and a post with no visible key is a post whose deletion cannot be explained — but it is a cost to the reading of the channel and it is on every post. And **no memory of a post the relay lost**: a post the owner deleted from Buzz Desktop before its suggestion expired, or one a rebuilt relay no longer holds, is one the clerk cannot tell from a suggestion it never posted, so a redelivered trigger for it is posted again. That is the right answer for a lost post and the wrong one for a deliberately removed one, and this ADR records that the clerk cannot distinguish the two and does not try: deciding on a suggestion — approving it, refusing it, and deleting its post for that reason — is [#284](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/284)'s, and it is a decision the Gateway records, which is where a fact like "the owner refused this" belongs and where the clerk can read it from rather than remember it.
