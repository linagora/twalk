# A contact's words live apart from the event that identifies them, and for a shorter time

The free text a **contact** wrote is published as its own event, on its own stream, and kept **seven days**; everything that identifies the message carrying it stays on `twalk` and is kept **ninety**. The line is not content against metadata, it is **whose words**: a contact's `data.body`, every quoted `excerpt` wherever one appears — including inside the owner's own message and their own reaction — a persona's `suggestion.body` and `rationale`, a contact's attachment `caption`, and an attachment's decryption material all move; the envelope, `contact.display_name`, the owner's own `data.body` on `outbound.message.sent`, the `final.body` of what was sent under their identity, an attachment's metadata (`mxc_uri`, `mime_type`, `size_bytes`, `dimensions`, `duration_ms`, `kind`) and the `reason` on the two operational types all stay. `data.contact.network_identifier` stops being published at all: it has no reader anywhere in the repository, and the cheapest way to protect a contact's phone number is not to hold it. One generic type carries the words and names the event they belong to, so the contract gains an eleventh type rather than one per text-bearing type — there are seven of those, and ten distinct field paths. The words are published **first** and the identity second, so an identity whose words never arrive cannot exist; the only surviving failure is an orphan words event nothing can join, which expires by itself. The identity event names where its words are and until when, so a consumer distinguishes a race from an expiry by construction rather than by guessing at a retention policy it does not read. For a `revoked` sender no words event is published at all, which makes the reduced shape the schema already has simpler rather than harder. The SDK hides the join, and the words type **cannot** be a trigger: it carries an event id and text, no subject, no network and no consent, so the allowlist that keeps a persona from being woken by the wrong thing does not widen by a single member.

Rationale: portal rooms are end-to-end encrypted and Synapse holds only ciphertext, so **the bus is the only place in the deployment where the plaintext of other people's messages exists at rest** — an exposure that arrived with `..Default::default()` at four separate stream-creation sites rather than with a decision, and which `docs/architecture/security-model.md` already calls the widest one. Measured on the reference deployment: 1,530 events in 27 hours, and a persona produced its suggestion **one second** after the message it answered, against a suggestion that is approvable for one hour. Nothing needs a contact's words for ninety days; seven is the shortest span that still lets an `archive` or `watch` persona be deployed and reason over the week just past, which is the only use that buys days at all. The alternative of simply shortening everything was rejected because it throws away what the identity is for: the consent screen can name a contact only from a name it reads on the bus, nothing stores that name by design, and a message-level history exists nowhere else — no projection holds one. Publishing nothing at all was rejected for a sharper reason than convenience: a persona's environment is a closed allowlist with no Matrix credential and the SDK contains no Matrix code, so the bus is a persona's only window on the world, and withholding the words does not reduce what is held, it turns the product off. The decision is **not retroactive**: events already published age out under the policy they were published with, because republishing them split would mean re-emitting a contact's words in order to shorten how long a contact's words are kept, and because purging a user's bus without asking destroys their history; an operator who wants it gone sooner has a purge.

The costs are accepted rather than argued away. Two publications per message and a join in the SDK, where before there was one event and no join — the complexity is real and it is concentrated in one place on purpose, beside the consent gate, for the same reason that one is there: a mechanism nobody can forget beats a documented rule. **The separation is explicit, not enforced.** The reference bus has no authentication at all, so a component that must not read a contact's words is one that does not subscribe rather than one that cannot; what the split genuinely buys is that forgetting to subscribe shows nothing, where forgetting not to read a field shows everything, and NATS accounts are named here as the available hardening so that nobody reads "structural" and believes it is already imposed. The eleventh type is spent before the v1.0 freeze, on the same argument [ADR 0018](0018-the-users-own-messages-are-their-own-event-type.md) made for the ninth. An attachment's decryption material moves with the words, which cuts the window in which every file a contact ever sent is decryptable from ninety days to seven — but the ciphertext itself sits in Synapse's media store with no retention at all, so that window is the whole of the protection, and since nothing in this repository reads `data.attachments`, this ADR records that the field should go entirely if nothing reads it by the freeze. And one thing this ADR does **not** fix, found while taking it: `DISPLAY_NAME_WINDOW` bounds the name lookup at a thousand messages rather than a duration, which was eighteen hours at the traffic measured before [#152](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/152) removed ninety-five per cent of it and is nearer a fortnight after — one constant meaning two things a factor of twenty apart, decided by traffic that has nothing to do with names. The bound stays, because the alternatives are an unbounded scan on every screen paint or a directory of everyone's name on disk, which is the thing that module exists not to build; what changes is that the answer names the horizon it actually reached, so a contact shown as a raw Matrix ID says why, the way [ADR 0024](0024-a-portal-room-is-observed-by-invitation-per-conversation.md) requires of the portal register.

**Amendment, 2026-09-24 ([#354](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/354)): what the owner decides to publish about their own data is published like anything else, and inherits this retention.**

A location that travels is a string on the bus for as long as the bus keeps the event, and it is a fact about a place the owner will be. That is the point of putting it under a recorded switch rather than a configuration flag: the owner is deciding about **retention** as much as about an agent's knowledge, and the settings screen says what opening it lets leave the machine.

Nothing here becomes a second class of data. A published location lives and expires with the event that carries it; turning the switch off does not unpublish what a consumer already holds, which the screen states before the press rather than after it. And the fields this ADR was written about — a contact's words — are untouched: the description of a meeting somebody else organised is theirs, it is never read, and no switch publishes it.
