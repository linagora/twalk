# A login's shape is the bridge's, and its words are ours

One component owns the **flow** of a bridge login — which step the deployment is on, what may be submitted, what happens when it ends — and delegates the *presentation* of each step kind to a component of its own, so the QR keeps the drawing code it already has and stops owning the sequence. Exhaustiveness is made real rather than asserted: the dispatch is a table typed over the view's own discriminant, so a step kind nobody draws is a **compile error**, with a residual panel for the day a Gateway speaks of a step this Companion does not know. Inside a step the renderer is driven by the **field type**: fields the bridge groups by type — a jar of `cookie` fields sharing a domain — are collected through one control and its parser, while ordinary fields get one control each; and a type that is **unknown or absent** refuses that field and names it on screen, rather than defaulting to a text input. A step's **words** are the project's wherever honesty needs more than a bridge says: keyed on the step id, **replacing** the bridge's instructions rather than appending to them, covering a step's failure copy as well as its instructions, and failing loudly when an overridden step's shape drifts instead of showing an explanation that has quietly gone stale. The step is **named and never counted**, because a bridge does not know how many steps remain until it knows the account. A refused answer is **its own outcome**: the bridge drops the login process when it refuses a value, so the renderer says the network refused it and that the login must be started again, carrying the Gateway's own sentence, and the step disappears instead of inviting a resubmit that would answer nothing. A reload **re-attaches** to the live process and re-renders the step it is actually at, with anything typed gone; an explicit abandon cancels the bridge's process, leaving the page does not, and a login left running is visible from the network's card. And the Gateway **enforces the login lifetime** it already documents. An answer is never stored and is unloggable by construction: the relay log keeps a payload's shape — how many members an object has — and not the length of any string in it.

Rationale: this is overdue rather than speculative. `user_input` is fully modelled in the Companion (`login-view.ts` carries a step's fields as a *list*, with the parse to match) and fully plumbed in the Gateway, which drives an arbitrary sequence and is exercised by the Rust suite — **and rendered by nothing**. `QrLogin.svelte` branches on six of the view's ten kinds and has no `{:else}`, so an `input`, `cookies` or `emoji` step draws an empty seventeen-rem box with no instructions and no way out, while the session polls it once a second for ever. A Telegram QR login by an account with two-factor authentication reaches that panel today, because the QR flow interjects a `password` step. The file's own header promised the opposite — *"adding one to the Gateway makes the screens fail to compile rather than fall through to a blank page"* — and a Svelte `{#if}` chain receives no exhaustiveness check, so the promise was aspirational; a typed dispatch table is what makes it true. A **purely generic** renderer, taking whatever the bridge says and rendering it by type with no Companion copy at all, was the tempting alternative and would have given new networks for free; it loses because it would have made [#57](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/57) impossible. mautrix's instructions for the Google cookie step are eleven words; what the user needs is why a private window is required, that signing out of a normal window kills the session the server is holding, that Chrome's Device Bound Session Credentials tie a session to the hardware key and must be off, and what the seven cookies together let their holder do. No bridge will ever say any of that, and a bridge explains its own mechanism rather than the user's exposure — the same reasoning [ADR 0019](0019-a-persona-discloses-itself-to-the-contact.md) used to make a persona's disclosure a sentence this project chooses rather than a prefix a bridge adds. Rendering one control per field was rejected for a reason the shipped copy already argues: people arrive at that step holding a whole `Cookie` header or an extension's export, not seven values to transcribe, and the field type the bridge itself sets is what makes "collect these together" a signal from the data rather than a special case for one network.

The costs are accepted rather than argued away. **The central case is untestable until the fixtures change**: neither the Node stub nor the Rust one can serve two `user_input` steps in a row — both complete the login on any submit that is not a blocking or cookie step — so a password-then-code sequence, a step re-issued after a refusal, and an unknown field type have no coverage and no fixture able to express them. That is a prerequisite of this work and not a follow-up, and this project has been bitten three times in one ticket by trusting a hand-written shape ([#106](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/106)). **Re-attaching on reload is real work, not an adjustment**: today neither screen reads the login's state on mount, both start a new one, the Gateway refuses it as a login already in flight without comparing devices, and the only way forward destroys the login — so a reload currently ends what it was meant to resume. **A per-step override can rot**: keying it on the step id and failing loudly when the shape drifts is a mitigation and not a cure, and a bridge that renames a step will take an explanation with it. **A refused answer costs the user a fresh code**, and nothing here can change that, because it is the bridge that drops the process. And enforcing the lifetime is the half of the decision that "leaving the page does not cancel" created: without a reaper, one abandoned step holds a bridge's only login slot indefinitely and every later attempt on that network is refused until a human takes over.
