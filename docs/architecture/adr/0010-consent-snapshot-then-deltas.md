# Consent is read from a Gateway snapshot, then followed on the bus

The Companion Gateway exposes the current consent state over HTTP; consumers read that snapshot at startup and follow `consent.state.changed` on the bus for everything after it. The snapshot names the stream sequence it reflects, and a consumer starting cold creates its durable consumer from that sequence plus one, so a decision is never applied twice or in the wrong order. Revocations appear explicitly in the snapshot — an absent subject means "no decision", never "revoked". The Gateway's own store is the record of truth (an append-only journal, current state as its projection), published through a transactional outbox; the bus is the audit trail, not the memory.

Rationale: the Sensor's consent cache is in memory, and a durable consumer resumes at its ack floor, so a restart silently downgraded granted contacts to `pending` (issue #16). Three alternatives were weighed. Replaying the whole stream makes a confidentiality guarantee expire with a retention policy. A NATS KV projection would make the bus a second holder of the truth, against ADR 0006's single writer. Per-subject version counters invent an ordering space when the stream already has one. Sequence-based hand-off keeps one gatekeeper, trusts no clock, and leaves the audit trail complete.
