# A conversation outlives its room, but the room stays the key

A conversation's identity is **not** its Matrix room id — a room can be replaced while the conversation continues — and the room id nevertheless remains what the portal register is keyed on and what an observation decision names. The two are reconciled by the **tombstone chain**: `m.room.tombstone` carries `replacement_room`, so a dead room *names* its successor and the register follows that pointer, which is why continuity needs no network identifier at all and therefore works for a native Matrix room exactly as for a bridged one. An observation decision **follows the conversation** when its room is replaced, except when the successor's audience crosses the threshold the user acknowledged when they chose it ([#143](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/143)), in which case the conversation returns to the chooser as a decision to take again — a threshold that applied only at the moment of ticking would be a threshold it suffices to wait out. The register gains a **fourth observation state** for a conversation that moved and whose successor the Sensor is not in, on the same argument ADR 0024 made for `invited`: without it the symptom is silence that nothing accounts for. A moved conversation appears **once**, at its successor, because a room whose tombstone exists holds no conversation and the register is a statement of where conversations are, not an inventory of rooms — two rows would make "the Sensor is outside 30 of your 32 conversations" false by one at every migration. The **register** notices the tombstone, not the Sensor, because "should this successor be followed?" is a consent question and consent has one writer; the Sensor's own part is to stop publishing from a dead room, which is a fact and not a decision, and to **leave** it once the successor is settled, since it is the Sensor's membership that the register reads as `observing`. And an observation decision is taken at the grain of the **room**, not the thread: a room may carry several conversations inside it, the interface must say that ticking a room ticks every thread in it including future ones, and `CONTEXT.md` stops claiming a portal room holds exactly one external conversation, because it does not.

Rationale: the conversation id **cannot** be the register's key, and this is a constraint rather than a preference. A native Matrix room has no `m.bridge` by construction, so it has a room id and nothing else, and that channel is shipped (ADR 0009, ADR 0020) — a conversation-keyed register could not cover it, leaving two observation mechanisms with two keys, which is the drift ADR 0024 set out to avoid. `network_conversation_id` is an `Option`: a bridge may write nothing, and a key that is absent for some rows is not a key. Its uniqueness is unestablished — nothing in the repository claims it, no test asserts it, and `portals.rs` insists the value is passed through untouched, so two bridges of one network under two logins could plausibly produce the same value. Most decisively, **`Observation` is irreducibly a room membership**: Matrix has no per-conversation membership, and the register's answer to "is this observed?" is a membership event nobody can forge or forget — the property that ADR 0024 rests on. Deciding at thread grain was weighed and rejected for exactly that reason: since a thread has no membership, observing one thread and not another would mean the Sensor joins the room and **filters at publication**, moving consent from a structural property to application code, and code that forgets to filter publishes. Room grain is also what the system already does, so making it explicit costs a glossary sentence and a sentence on screen rather than a rearchitecture of consent. The alternative of noticing tombstones in the Sensor was rejected because entering a room is not the Sensor's decision (ADR 0024, [#105](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/105)) and giving it back a decision that was deliberately taken away from it is a worse trade than five minutes of blindness between register reads.

The costs are accepted rather than argued away. **After a migration, nothing on the bus relates the conversation's two halves.** An event's id is `sha256(matrix_event_id + ':' + matrix_room_id)`, so a room change changes every id downstream, and folding a conversation id into that derivation would break deduplication and the identity of ten event types for a need nobody has expressed; following the chain of rooms is a query concern, and the homeserver holds the chain. **Threads are not separately decidable**, which is a real gap in the per-conversation promise and not a hypothetical one: the Sensor already parses `m.thread` and publishes `thread_root`, and `sensor/tests/fidelity.rs` proves it end to end, so the bus distinguishes conversations that the register and the chooser cannot — and the crowd acknowledgement is therefore computed against the room's headcount, which is the right number for who can read what the Sensor publishes and the wrong number for the sub-conversation a user may believe they are ticking. The glossary's claim that a portal room holds exactly one external conversation was **false when it was written**, and a reader who trusted it would have built on it; this ADR is also the record of that correction. Five minutes of blindness after a migration is the register's refresh interval, and it is nameable in the interface rather than silent. And a conversation that moved is reported even when it was followed automatically, because a deployment that changed rooms under the user without ever being able to say so is one whose history they cannot check — ADR 0024's posture that the register's purpose is as much to state a number as to change one.
