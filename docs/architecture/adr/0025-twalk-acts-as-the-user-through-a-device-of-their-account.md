# Twalk acts as the user through a device of their own account

Wherever Twalk must act as the user rather than observe them, it does so through a **device of the user's own Matrix account**, created during onboarding and held in the Sensor's store. Two cases, one mechanism: an approved reply is posted into a portal room as the user, so the bridge relays it because it really is the user ([#123](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/123)); and an account on a homeserver the deployment does not drive is observed by that device's own sync rather than by inviting `@sensor:<deployment>` into rooms it would have to federate to reach (ADR 0020). The credential never passes through the Companion Gateway, which continues to hold no Matrix access token (ADR 0011) — that rule now names where the token *does* live instead of leaving a reader to infer that nowhere means nowhere. The device is cross-signed like any other of the user's, appears in their device list, and is revocable from any Matrix client without Twalk's involvement.

Rationale: a bridge relays to its network only what the logged-in user's own Matrix account sends, and Twalk had nothing that could be that account — the Sensor's messages were ignored without a log line, and the user was only ever *invited* to their portal rooms, never joined. Found by sending a message through a live Signal portal and watching a phone receive nothing while Synapse returned an event id and every component reported itself healthy. Two alternatives were weighed. mautrix's own double puppeting needs nothing invented, which is a real argument; it loses because it gives the *bridge* the power to act as the user, and because it answers only the bridged case, leaving ADR 0020's foreign homeserver to a second mechanism — one question, two machines, drifting apart from the day they are written. Relay mode is cheaper still and adds no credential at rest, and it loses on something other than cost: the bridge prefixes the relayed message in its own words, while ADR 0019 already decided that a persona's disclosure to a contact is a sentence this project chooses; a bridge's `[Twalk]` and our own sentence would contradict each other in the same message.

The costs are accepted rather than argued away. A long-lived credential for the user's own account exists at rest, and a compromised host yields it — mitigated only by its being revocable from any client the user already has, and by its being a device among their devices rather than a hidden grant. Cross-signing is the hard part and it is the same one ADR 0020 named: a new device reads encrypted history only once signed by the account's own identity, which on an SSO-only account means a verification the user performs from another of their clients, and which no automation should attempt to shortcut by asking for a recovery key. And the capability this creates is broader than the use decided here: Twalk can now write to third parties under the user's identity, which is precisely what informing a contact would require ([#122](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/122)) and precisely why that question stays open rather than being answered by the existence of the means.
