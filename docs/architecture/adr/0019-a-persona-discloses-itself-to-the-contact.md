# A persona discloses itself to the contact, by default

Every reply a persona drafted carries a short disclosure to the person receiving it — a line stating that the message was written with the user's assistant and sent by the user. It is on by default, the user can turn it off, and turning it off is recorded as a deliberate act with a timestamp rather than a silent preference. The disclosure is part of the outbound message, so it reaches the contact through whatever network the conversation is on, and it is translated into the language the reply is written in (ADR 0016), not the user's. A message the user wrote themselves carries nothing: the disclosure states a fact about how *this* message was produced, and attaching it to everything would make it meaningless.

Rationale: the audit trail already records who approved a reply, and that trail serves the operator — the one person who does not need telling. The contact, who is interacting with an AI system without knowing it, had nothing. That asymmetry is not defensible for a European product, where Article 50 of the AI Act requires a person be informed they are interacting with an AI system, and it is not defensible on Twalk's own terms either: a project that reduces what it publishes about a revoked contact, and refuses to send a reply whose consent lapsed a second earlier, cannot also decide that the person on the other end has no interest in knowing. The alternative of disclosing once per conversation was rejected because "once" becomes "never" over a long relationship and a contact who joins a group later never sees it. Disclosing nothing at all — arguing the human read, edited and approved, so the message is theirs — is coherent and was rejected: it is a position that would have to be defended publicly one day, and the defence would be that we chose the reading most convenient to us. Making the disclosure non-removable was rejected in turn, because a user who cannot turn it off will stop using the suggestion feature, or paste the text by hand, and a disclosure that pushes people into a worse path discloses nothing. Default on, removable, and the removal on the record is the honest shape of that trade-off.

Implemented by [#121](https://github.com/linagora/twalk/issues/121) on 2026-09-20; the shape it took — where the sentence lives, who selects it, who appends it, and where turning it off is recorded — is [ADR 0031](0031-the-disclosure-is-written-by-neither-the-model-nor-the-gateway.md) and its amendment.
